# Where the ideas come from

Yoyū is an organising tool, not a treatment, and it has not been tested in a clinical study. Its three modules follow ideas that are well described in research and in what autistic people write about their own lives. Reading these is the best way to judge, adapt or criticise them.

## Worry time (module "Worries")

- **Postponing worry to a fixed daily time** is one of the oldest behavioural techniques for chronic worry: Borkovec, T. D., Wilkinson, L., Folensbee, R., & Lerman, C. (1983). Stimulus control applications to the treatment of worry. *Behaviour Research and Therapy*, 21(3), 247–251.
- **Sorting worries into current, solvable problems and hypothetical "what ifs"**, and practising tolerance of uncertainty for the second kind, comes from the intolerance-of-uncertainty model of generalised anxiety developed in Québec: Dugas, M. J., Gagnon, F., Ladouceur, R., & Freeston, M. H. (1998). Generalized anxiety disorder: A preliminary test of a conceptual model. *Behaviour Research and Therapy*, 36(2), 215–226. A clinician's manual: Robichaud, M., Koerner, N., & Dugas, M. J. (2019). *Cognitive Behavioral Treatment for Generalized Anxiety Disorder: From Science to Practice* (2nd ed.). Routledge.
- **Checking later whether a worry came true** is a common self-observation exercise in cognitive behavioural work. Yoyū only counts the answers; it draws no conclusion for the person.
- Guidelines that recommend cognitive behavioural therapy for generalised anxiety in adults: NICE, *Generalised anxiety disorder and panic disorder in adults: management* (CG113).

## Energy log (module "Battery")

- Many autistic adults describe a limited daily budget of energy that sensory load, social effort and change use up. A widely shared description of limited energy is Christine Miserandino's essay *The Spoon Theory* (2003).
- Running beyond that budget for long is linked to autistic burnout: Raymaker, D. M., et al. (2020). "Having all of your internal resources exhausted beyond measure and being left with no clean-up crew": Defining autistic burnout. *Autism in Adulthood*, 2(2), 132–143.
- "Masking", hiding one's difficulties to look at ease, is described as tiring: Hull, L., et al. (2017). "Putting on my best normal": Social camouflaging in adults with autism spectrum conditions. *Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders*, 47(8), 2519–2534. It is one of the tags.
- **How patterns are shown.** No pattern before 14 logged days. For each tag seen on at least 5 days and missing on at least 5, a permutation test compares the mean energy of both groups; the threshold is divided by the number of tags tested, so that the list stays short and chance rarely puts something on it. A test checks this: on 60 logs of pure noise, at most 3 show a pattern. Even then, a pattern is presented as a clue, not a cause.

## Preparing situations (module "Prepare")

Knowing what will happen, having words ready and a plan B are common supports for autistic adults facing calls, appointments, meetings, trips and changes of plan. The templates are starting points written in plain language; each person edits them to fit their own life.

## What is not done, on purpose

- No streaks, scores, badges or reminders that make a missed day feel like a failure.
- No notification by default: the app waits to be opened.
- No advice that relies on physical discomfort.
- No interpretation of the person's notes beyond the counts and patterns described above.
